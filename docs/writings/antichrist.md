*We Christians may hope for neither.*

Emperor Nero was accused of being the Antichrist as early as the second century — his name rendered in Hebrew gematria yielded 666, and *Revelation* was written within decades of his suicide.[^nero] The Papacy was accused — so declared Luther, Calvin, Tyndale, and Cotton Mather, a conviction enshrined in Protestant confessional writings for centuries.[^papacy] Frederick II Hohenstaufen, *Stupor Mundi*, was accused — excommunicated three times, the last condemnation naming him the Great Beast.[^frederick] Napoleon was accused — Tsar Alexander called him "the Anti-Christ and the enemy of God," and Russian Orthodox clergy told their peasants the French were "a legion of devils commanded by the Anti-Christ."[^napoleon] In Tolstoy's *War and Peace*, Pierre discovers through cipher that *Le Empereur Napoléon* sums to 666 — then discovers that his own name, rendered awkwardly as *L'Russe Bestuhof*, also makes 666, and resolves that he must be the one to destroy Napoleon.[^tolstoy] Hitler, Mussolini, Stalin, Gorbachev, Kissinger, nearly every American president from FDR to Obama — the accusation of being the Antichrist was the most serious opprobrium in Christendom, and it was deployed constantly.[^sutton]

Then we stopped.

In our ever-secular world, the discussion has altogether dropped. The Antichrist was once the most discussed figure in Christendom. For centuries, Christians talked about him constantly — accused each other of *being* him — and the discourse, whatever its excesses, kept a certain vigilance alive. Anything we used to talk about this much, that we no longer discuss at all, is worth considering.

*The spirit of the Antichrist arose in the bosom of the Church and works within it like weeds in a wheat field. "They went out from us!" (1 John 2:19). The apostasy of the last days is intra-Christian. Both the contemporary church and the reactionary traditionalist churches seem to confirm this from opposite ends. The contemporary church dilutes liturgy into self-celebration — "the community celebrates itself," as Ratzinger observed.[^ratzinger] The traditional church calcifies into spiritual pride and insularity. This is not a doctrinal complaint against the latter — they hold to the divine sonship — but it is a different kind of problem, and both testify to the spirit working from within. "Self-destruction of the Church" (Paul VI). "Silent apostasy" (John Paul II). "Evaporation of the faith" (Benedict XVI).[^popes] The truly believing among the baptized have become a small minority. The question is not when the Antichrist will appear, but whether the characteristics of the Antichrist can be recognized in the present.*

## The Promise Without God

> *"But what is the absolute meaning of this drama? I still do not understand why the Anti-Christ hates God so much, while he himself is essentially good, not evil. ... That is the point. He is not* essentially *evil. All the meaning is in that. I take back my previous words that 'You cannot explain the Anti-Christ by proverbs alone.' He can be explained by a simple proverb: 'all that glitters is not gold.'"*[^solovyov]

The promise of the Antichrist is the promise of God, without God. Achieving all that alignment with God's will promises — peace, mercy, justice, equality — without God. The pinnacle of our secular perversion of virtue and justice. In Solovyov's telling, the Antichrist "believed in God, but in the depths of his soul he involuntarily and unconsciously preferred himself."[^solovyov] He realizes the Christian ideal in secularized form: the Cross removed, the dogma hollowed out, the word used not to make reality known but as a means of power. And the word of Christ is fulfilled:

> *"I have come in my Father's name, and you do not accept me. Another will come in his own name — him you will accept."*
> — *John* 5:43

For it is necessary to be *agreeable* to be *accepted*.

It becomes ever clearer that humanity can build our pyre ever higher, pursuing peace, mercy, justice, equality. And yet without the spark of God to light our pyre ablaze, we are left in the perverted cold. Just as a cold, unlit stack of wood provides no warmth to man, regardless of how tall the pile of logs has been tediously stacked, so too our acts of "faith" and "love" give us nothing without the spark of God — without the hope that our pyre will be lit, that we will experience our warmest warm.

Faith, Hope, and Love are discussed as the key tenets of the Church, but all too often it seems to bode as Faith-Hope and Love. If one holds Faith and Hope interchangeable — when we view the faithful via the naïve lens of simply those who have faith — we forgo a substantial element of religion. It is in Hope which we are saved.[^spesalvi] Faith and Love alone are tractable requests, which is why I believe contemporary humans — both believers and non-believers — to be initially attracted to them. You can do X, Y, and Z things, and build your pyre ever closer to God. But without the hope that our pyre will be lit, we are left stacking logs with our eyes on the ground. The secular humanist project, in this reading, is just competitive log-stacking.

*In the Thomistic tradition, it is Love (caritas) that is called the "form of the virtues" — the thing that animates Faith and makes it alive rather than dead. Faith without Love is fides informis, unformed faith.[^aquinas] I do not argue against this hierarchy. Love is the spark itself — God's love for us, which we receive. Hope is the orientation toward the spark — the upward gaze, the expectation that the pyre will be lit. Faith stacks the logs. Hope turns your face toward the sky. Love is the fire. Without Hope, you stack logs with your eyes on the ground, and when the fire comes you don't even see it.*

Why would Aquinas have spent so much time on Love? I imagine because the dogma of the Church at his time was lacking it, while it is clearly stated in God's word. I do not intend to underwrite Love's importance, but rather to perform the same work as Aquinas: when we look to the Church Fathers, the word of Our Lord, what is most absent in our contemporary discourse? I conjecture this to be Hope. Each era of the Church has a virtue-deficit, and the theologian's task is to diagnose which one. God wants to light our fire, He wants to love us, and this seems to be accepted. Nobody in the contemporary Church needs to be told that God loves them. That message has been received — possibly over-received, possibly distorted into therapeutic self-affirmation. The contemporary resolution is that one may accept God's love through faith alone. But to truly appreciate the warmth of the fire, we must hope for its warmth.

The Antichrist is the greatest pyre-builder in human history. He delivers peace. He eliminates poverty. He offers bread and games. As Solovyov writes: "The most fundamental form of equality was firmly established among humankind, the *equality of universal satiety*."[^solovyov] He stacks the pyre higher than anyone thought possible. And people accept it because they have lost the capacity to hope for actual fire. They have forgotten what real warmth is. They have settled.

And it is cold.

*The Antichrist brings a pseudo-peace without God: unity at the expense of truth, peace and security at the expense of freedom — the totalitarian world state. "War was always something terrible… yet it seemed to Percy as if there could be something worse. What should one think of a world peace — a peace that was not the peace of Jesus Christ?"[^benson] "Every love of fatherland killed," as Donoso Cortés warned.[^donoso] There is something to be said about the falsifiers of words being near the bottom of Dante's hell. In Inferno Canto 30, Sinon burns in Malebolge for the lie that destroyed Troy: "If I spake false, thou falsifiedst the coin — and for one fault I am here, and thou for more than any other demon." Yet even lower, in Canto 32, are those who have betrayed their country — frozen in Antenora, closer to Lucifer than the liars above.[^dante] The one world state would unite both sins: the corruption of language and the betrayal of one's country. Extrinsic threat forces power into centralized governments, makes nationalism seem unfavorable, as the alternative to one world is armageddon. Solovyov captures the mechanism: "If the half-century of submission to the Asiatic barbarians was due to the disunity of the European States which had concerned themselves only with their own national interests, a great and glorious independence was achieved by an international organization of the united forces of the entire European population."[^solovyov] The post-WWII education of Germany and Japan offers a striking illustration. Germany has taken the role of systematic self-deprecation — a perverse hatred of their own people and country instilled in the young. Japan does not teach of their imperial conquest in primary school at all. The result: a strong sense of nationalism persists among young Japanese, producing a cultural soil in which a work like Eiichiro Oda's One Piece — a band of pirates fighting against the world government — could emerge. You could not begin to imagine this coming out of Germany.*

## The Infinite Delta

Consider our scene of Jesus in the temple, flipping over tables, claiming that they have disgraced *his Father's* house. How absolutely ludicrous! In fact this is such an absurd scene that it demands a polarity: regardless of how morally good Jesus was, regardless of how faithful and loving his teachings are, he is claiming to be the Son of God. And so either he is the Son of God, or he is not, and if he is not, the ridiculousness of this claim washes over all possible, empirical goods done by Jesus.

*This is structurally Lewis's trilemma — liar, lunatic, or Lord — redeployed.[^lewis] The claim to divine sonship creates a binary that no quantity of temporal good can resolve. Jesus's empirical goodness is irrelevant to the question of his claim. If the claim is true, everything follows. If false, no amount of good teaching or moral example rescues him from the absurdity of claiming divine sonship in his Father's house.*

If the Antichrist claims himself the highest good, the most just, but is in fact not, we may cast the same judgment. Even if the Antichrist proves to be the *second* most just, most virtuous, behind only our Lord himself, the claim that he is, in fact, the *first* is as evil and terrible as the delta between the justness and virtue of the Antichrist and Our Lord — which is infinite. Making this claim the furthest possible thing from Christianity.

The gap between any finite good and God is not a gap of degree but of kind. It is infinite. So the second most just being, in claiming primacy, does not commit a small error. He commits the maximal possible sin. The closer he gets to the good while refusing God, the worse it is. This is why Solovyov's Antichrist is essentially good and yet the devil's masterpiece.

*Solovyov describes the Antichrist as one who "believed in Good, but the All Seeing Eye of the Eternal knew that this man would bow down before the power of Evil as soon as it would offer him a bribe — not by deception of the senses and the lower passions, not even by the superior bait of power, but only by his own immeasurable self-love." "In a word, he considered himself to be what Christ in reality was."[^solovyov] The Antichrist may be viewed as the inverse of John the Baptist: both predate the coming of Christ; one prepares Christ's way, the other claims it for his own. "As a moralist, Christ divided humanity by the notion of good and evil. The Antichrist shall unite it by benefits which are as much needed by good as by evil people." "Christ brought the sword; the Antichrist shall bring peace."[^solovyov]*

Why is Plato's conclusion in the *Meno* that we must be numb like the torpedo fish to understand virtue? Because it is not for us to understand. Plato does not merely conclude that virtue is mysterious — he concludes that it comes by *theia moira*, divine dispensation. Virtue is not a human achievement. It arrives.[^plato] This maps directly onto the pyre: you do not light your own fire. And the Antichrist's fundamental error is claiming that you can.

The ultimate perversion of our consumption from the tree of knowledge is the hubris that we may cast the final judgment ourselves — that we may find a system of virtue robust enough to achieve peace and safety for the human race. And this is necessarily perverted. Because if you consider the most empirically possible highest virtue, highest justice, still you may imagine one higher.

*Newman warns: "Far be it from us to be seduced with the fair promises in which Satan is sure to hide his poison! He promises you civil liberty; he promises you equality; he promises you trade and wealth… He shows you how to become as gods."[^newman]*

## Hope

> *"'SPE SALVI facti sumus' — in hope we were saved."*[^spesalvi]

Why did we stop discussing the Antichrist? Because society became more secular. And if so, how much more important is it to reinspire that discussion now. But something else disappeared alongside it. We stopped hoping. We stopped hoping as our primitive hopes were achieved: we have been in the longest period of general peace and prosperity ever, and we are beginning to achieve what a naïve person would perceive to be the promise of God. The decline of Antichrist discourse and the decline of Hope are the same phenomenon viewed from two angles — the pyre got tall enough that people mistook the height of the logs for warmth. Why did we stop hoping? Because some period exists where our primitive, perverse hopes are resolved? How much more important is it to consider the limit of what hope could be.

Imagine the most blissful thing you have ever experienced. Now imagine an experience that is that level of bliss, plus one. You can do this, but it is not empirically grounded — you have never experienced something that blissful, yet you may imagine something more blissful than the most blissful thing you have experienced. If you take the limit of this, the *most* blissful, the *most* good — this is what we hope for. And we may have faith in its existence through the fact that some amount of bliss, some amount of good, spill over into our lives. The capacity to conceive of greater good than we have received is not empirically bounded. That capacity is Hope operating in us.

*This construction is structurally Anselmian — "that than which nothing greater can be conceived" — applied to experienced goodness rather than logical necessity.[^anselm]*

The Antichrist's project is to cap that series. To say: *this* is the most blissful. This peace, this prosperity, this humanitarian achievement — this is the ceiling. Stop reaching. The Antichrist does not tell you there is no good. He tells you *this* is the good. He satisfies the plus-one with temporal goods so you stop taking the limit. The seduction is not the denial of bliss but the finitude of it. The pyre is the ceiling. The Antichrist offers warmth without fire — temporal comfort, secular morality, humanitarian progress. It *feels* warm.

*There is a valid reading that AGI may fulfill this role — providing both peace and safety and hyper-personalized entertainment for each human within its permanent underclass. Solovyov: "But if satisfaction is a question of primary importance for the hungry, the satisfied ones crave for something else. Even satiated animals usually want not only to sleep but also to play — the more so with humanity which has always post panem craved for circenses."[^solovyov] A real or fictitious display of miracles will strengthen the Antichrist's power, and accelerating technological progress may be read into this. Man will not know the time or the hour, and I will abstain from hubris.*

Hope is the refusal to accept a finite ceiling on the good.

Faith tells you what is coming. Hope is the active orientation toward God's will *despite* what is coming. The fatalists are necessarily wrong. They submit to the Faith-Hope and Love bucket. They may hold the faith properly, and submit the coming of a man of sin as a necessary precondition before the return of Christ, yet they do not hope. Faith without Hope collapses into mere intellectual assent to prophecy. It becomes passive, melancholic, resigned. Without Hope, Faith is just a calendar of predicted horrors. As Benedict writes: "Faith is not merely a personal reaching out towards things to come that are still totally absent: it gives us something. It gives us even now something of the reality that we are waiting for."[^spesalvi] Hope is the mechanism by which that drawing-in happens.

One may hope that humans achieve alignment with God's will without necessarily enduring the Antichrist. One in fact *should* hope, in the face of torment or an arduous life, or even facing the Antichrist himself: "I am definitively loved and whatever happens to me — I am awaited by this Love. And so my life is good."[^spesalvi]

Hope in this case does not necessarily delay the Antichrist, or stop its coming. We cannot be certain of God's will. Yet we must hope ourselves to align to it, actively. To work to allow God to love us. Without Hope we are melancholically "faithful," if you can even call that faith at all.

I hold it to be a reasonable conclusion that Jesus had hoped he hadn't needed to die. Had the apostles stayed awake per the request of Our Lord, maybe that hope would have yielded resolve. Yet they did not heed the request. The apostles *knew* what Christ told them was coming. They had Faith in the fullest sense — they had walked with him. But they fell asleep. They failed in Hope. And the Church keeps falling asleep at the moment vigilance matters most. Let us not fall asleep again. Let us stay awake in hope of our salvation.

Faithful Christians hold true the word of Our Lord and in tandem must hope our human world not be subjected to the Antichrist — just as the apostles, had they stayed the course and not fallen asleep that night, may have hoped for the saving of Jesus. Not contradicting what was ordained, but holding vigil against the darkness.

In place of polemicism, let us witness to a better way: "humility at all times about each of our own Traditions, charity towards one another now in all our dealings, even our theological exchanges, and hope for a future that — like it or not — will put all things in proper perspective and that we will inevitably share."[^solovyov]

The fight for the good seems to be in vain from the outset. One must nevertheless wage it, "for on the one hand the fight can postpone the catastrophe, on the other hand the fight is a duty and not a speculation… Let us thank God that he granted us the fight, and let us not demand, beyond the grace of the fight, the grace of triumph."[^fight]

*The Church goes not so much toward the Antichrist and his terrible rule, but rather looks out for the Lord, whom she expects coming on the clouds "seated at the right hand of the Almighty" (Mark 14:62) with "great power and glory" (Mark 13:26). Newman: "Just as the types of Christ went before Christ, so the shadows of Antichrist precede him."[^newman] The Fathers "do not speak of their own private opinion; they do not say, 'This is true, because we see it in Scripture' — about which there might be difference of judgment — but, 'this is true, because in matter of fact it is held, and has ever been held, by all the Churches, down to our times, without interruption, ever since the Apostles.'"[^newman]*

Gregory's admonition is bracing — but hope, properly understood, is not friendship with the world. It is friendship with the people still in it.
